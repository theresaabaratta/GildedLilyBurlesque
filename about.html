How It All Began

The Origin Story of
Baltimore's Gilded Lily

The roots of Gilded Lily Burlesque reach back to the early 2000s, when Baltimore's first wave of the neo-burlesque revival was just beginning to bloom. Trailblazer Trixie Little and her partner Evil Hate Monkey lit up intimate stages around Charm City, conjuring something that felt both historical and radical — the glamour and tease of classic burlesque, reimagined as a modern art form.

"At the time, I remember wanting the quaintness of a burlesque show in an intimate space," Trixie would later recall. "It seemed so romantic, but you couldn't find it anywhere, so we started creating the work we wanted to see." Through their own performances and burlesque bootcamp classes, Trixie and Monkey birthed a new generation of Baltimore performers — and among their most devoted students was a dancer, musician, and natural-born producer named Maria Bella.

"I fell in love. It was funny and theatrical.
I knew I needed to start my own troupe." — Maria Bella, Founder of Gilded Lily Burlesque

In 2007, Maria launched Gilded Lily Burlesque — and what began as one woman's passion project quickly grew into Baltimore's most celebrated, longest-running burlesque troupe. Nearly two decades later, Gilded Lily has evolved through multiple iterations, assembled a sisterhood of extraordinary performers, earned national acclaim, and established itself as a permanent fixture in Baltimore's creative identity.

Baltimore's Burlesque Legacy

Long before Gilded Lily, Baltimore had its own burlesque history. The city's legendary "The Block" — a stretch of East Baltimore Street — housed a thriving burlesque and vaudeville scene in the early-to-mid 20th century, making Charm City one of the great burlesque cities of its era.

By the time that glitter faded, The Block had given way to strip clubs and vacant storefronts. But Baltimore's love of performance never died. When the neo-burlesque revival of the 1990s arrived, Baltimore was primed to lead it — and Gilded Lily became the standard-bearer of that revival in Charm City.

The Art Form

What Is Neo-Burlesque?

noun, often adjective
Derived from Romance languages, the term originally referred to a theatrical production that used exaggeration and mockery to ridicule a serious subject. In America, burlesque evolved into a distinct art form combining comedy, theater, striptease, magic, drag, and variety performance.

Burlesque dwindled in the 1960s and 70s, but returned with force in the 1990s through the neo-burlesque movement — a reinvention that embraced the old traditions of satin and skin, then fused them with theatrical story lines, variety show elements, fierce feminist politics, and plenty of off-kilter humor.

More Than a Strip Show

Neo-burlesque is not pornography, and it's not a strip club. It is a form of performance art that uses the body as a medium for storytelling, social commentary, and radical self-expression. As Gilded Lily's GiGi Holliday has said: "Burlesque is my own protest."

What makes Gilded Lily's burlesque unique is the same thing that makes Baltimore unique: it is underdog, DIY, and deeply personal. Performers design and sew their own costumes — sometimes spending hundreds of hours on five minutes of stage time. Every act is a collaboration between craftsmanship and courage.

"Burlesque can really be anything you deem interesting, weird, or beautiful," says troupe member Nona Narcisse. "It's this wonderful adult art project." Acts range from classic striptease and cabaret homage to avant-garde theater, political commentary, live music integration, and circus-style variety performance.

Maria Bella
Founder · CEO · Producer · Performer

Maria Bella is a musician, award-winning variety performer, and touring producer based in Baltimore, MD. She founded Gilded Lily Burlesque in 2007 after falling in love with the art form at Trixie Little's legendary Burlesque Bootcamp.

Her acts blend classic cabaret, neo-burlesque, cultural imagery, exotic flair, fantasy, and world dance technique — delivered with the flair of a trumpet virtuoso who also happens to know exactly how to work a room.

The Gilded Lily School of Burlesque

Beyond performing, Maria Bella has spent years teaching the art of burlesque at Creative Alliance and other Baltimore venues. Of the roughly 50 students she has taught, over 90% have gone on to perform — many producing their own shows and founding their own troupes. Notable graduates include Sophia Sunday, who became a core Gilded Lily member, and Tapitha Kix, who went on to found Twisted Knickers Burlesque. Maria's teaching philosophy: burlesque builds confidence, self-acceptance, and courage — for performers of all shapes, sizes, races, and genders.

What Makes Gilded Lily Different

The Troupe's DNA

No two Gilded Lily performers are alike. That's the point.

🎭
Theatrical Storytelling

Every act is a narrative. Whether it's a tribute to Quentin Tarantino, a political statement inspired by the Baltimore Uprising, or a comedic Carol Burnett impression — Gilded Lily brings story to the strip.

🌈
Radical Inclusivity

Tall and short, skinny and full-bodied, Italian and Black and Irish, with a dash of Native American and Jewish. Brunette, redheaded, fuchsia-haired. Tattooed, pierced, loud, and quiet. Every body on the Gilded Lily stage belongs there.

✊
Art as Activism

Inspired by the Baltimore Uprising and the political moment of the 2016 election, Gilded Lily has used the stage to advocate for criminal justice reform, donate to the Innocence Project, and amplify the voices of BIPOC and LGBTQ+ performers.

🧵
Handmade from the Heart

Costumes are designed, sewn, and bedazzled by the performers themselves — every rhinestone, every feather, every tasseled pasty is an act of creation. The glitter you see on stage represents hundreds of hours of invisible labor.

🤝
Sisterhood & Collaboration

"Art is collaboration for us," Maria has said. "We have all become friends and a real sisterhood." Gilded Lily performers describe the troupe as family — the Justice League, the A-Team. They don't just share a stage; they share a life.

🏙️
Deeply Baltimore

Gilded Lily is not a touring production that rolled into Baltimore — it was born here, shaped by the city's creative culture, its history, and its neighborhoods, from Hampden to Highlandtown to Station North. Baltimore made Gilded Lily. Gilded Lily makes Baltimore proud.

What Drives Us

Mission & Values

From its earliest days, Gilded Lily Burlesque has operated with a mission that goes beyond entertainment. The troupe's official statement says it best:

"Our mission is to bring you entertainment that is fun and thought provoking. Come help us celebrate beauty, wit, power, sexuality and freedom."
💪
Body Positivity

Every shape, size, and age belongs on the Gilded Lily stage. There is no body shaming here — only celebration.

🏳️‍🌈
LGBTQ+ Visibility

The troupe is committed to giving LGBTQ+ performers a platform and the visibility they deserve in burlesque and variety arts.

✊🏾
BIPOC Representation

Gilded Lily actively works to combat the lack of visibility for performers of color in Baltimore burlesque — on stage and in its community.

🗣️
Social Discourse

From the Innocence Project to the Baltimore Uprising, the troupe uses the stage as a platform for the issues that matter most to Baltimore.

🤲
Consent Culture

"Laugh out loud, cheer, hoot and holler — and always obtain permission before interacting with performers." Consent is non-negotiable, always.

🎓
Education & Mentorship

Through the Gilded Lily School of Burlesque and community workshops, Maria Bella has trained the next generation of Baltimore performers for over a decade.

🌆
Community First

Gilded Lily exists to serve Baltimore's creative community — by producing shows, developing artists, and preserving the city's long performance heritage.

🎨
Artistic Freedom

No two acts are alike. No style is off-limits. Classic, avant-garde, comedic, political, erotic, absurdist — all are welcome under the Gilded Lily spotlight.
